Three steps from drawings to a number

No setup and no templates to build. Bring the plans you already have.

1

Upload your plans

Drop in the DOB-approved set as PDF or images. Add a CAD file (.dwg or .dxf) when you want exact wall lengths.

  • Large files are fine. Each plan is compressed in your browser first.
  • Best results come from the cover and zoning sheet, floor plans and the window, door and MEP schedules.
  • No plans yet? Describe the project in a sentence and start from that.
2

Check what it read

Floor areas, units, floors, perimeter and schedule counts land in one review screen. Fix anything that is off before it is priced.

  • Every field is editable.
  • Gross and net area per floor come from the plan's area table or your own figures.
  • Nothing is assumed. A missing value is shown as missing.
3

Get the estimate

A line-by-line takeoff with labor, crews and a timeline, then your overhead, profit, contingency and margin on top.

  • Edit any quantity or unit rate and the totals update at once.
  • Download Excel, print, or issue a client proposal.
  • Save the estimate and reopen it later.

Know what you are getting

It is a pre-bid tool, and it says so on every estimate. That is why you can trust the rest.

  • Accuracy is plus or minus 20–30% before bid. Schedule items are counted. Area and length materials such as drywall, partitions, paint and insulation are derived with standard takeoff factors.
  • Soft costs are not included. Design fees, DOB filing and inspection fees, financing, insurance and furniture are outside the estimate.
  • Check it against the dimensioned drawings before you bid. The review screen is there so you can correct what it read.

Questions

What files can I upload?

PDF or images (PNG or JPG). You can also add a CAD file, .dwg or .dxf, for exact wall lengths. For the best result include the cover and zoning sheet, the floor plans and the window, door and MEP schedules.

Can I start without plans?

Yes. Describe the project, for example a five-story multifamily in Queens with 24 units and two elevators, and generate a first estimate from the description. You can also enter the building metrics by hand.

How accurate is it?

Plus or minus 20–30% before bid, which is stated on every estimate. Items on the plan schedules are counted. Area and length materials are derived with standard takeoff factors. Verify against the dimensioned drawings before you bid.

Where do the prices come from?

A 2025–26 New York market price table, which you can adjust to the latest BLS producer price index. You can also import your own past budgets and price from those instead. Loaded trade wages are editable, with open-shop and union options.

Which areas does it cover?

The five boroughs of New York City and New Jersey counties, including Hudson, Essex, Union, Bergen, Passaic, Middlesex, Morris, Somerset, Monmouth, Mercer, Ocean, Camden and Burlington.

What is not in the estimate?

Soft costs: design fees, DOB filing and inspection fees, financing, insurance and furniture, fixtures and equipment.
